# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Owners, principals, and producers at US surety bond agencies (5–25 staff). They arrive skeptical and busy — they've seen generic insurance software before and they can smell a pitch from ten feet away. What converts them is recognition: the page has to describe their exact daily pain (retyping the same bond data into four documents, chasing renewals on an Excel tab, digging through email threads for a bond from last year) in terms specific enough to feel like the writer has done the work themselves. Most will read this on a phone from a LinkedIn link.

## Product Purpose

SuretyOS is a demand-validation and lead-capture landing page for a surety bond agency operating system that doesn't exist yet. The page has one job: capture qualified surety agencies as founding members before the product is built. Success is measured by qualified signups (agencies that write real bond volume) and the conversations those signups unlock — not raw traffic or vanity leads. The primary CTA is founding-agency signup (email capture + qualification fields). The secondary goal is communicating the product vision clearly enough that an agency owner immediately recognizes their own pain.

## Brand Personality

Confident, plain-spoken, industry-credible. Stripe and Mercury are the right visual register: structured, whitespace-heavy, financial-grade trust, navy-grounded. The copy talks like someone who knows surety — not adapted from P&C or general insurance, not startup-speak. Three words: expert, direct, credible.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS startup pages:** Cream or beige body backgrounds, emoji bullet lists, buzzword-heavy copy ("streamline," "empower," "seamless"), the 2024–2026 AI landing page aesthetic. This is the hardest trap because it is the default output.
- **Generic insurance portals:** The Applied Epic / EZLynx aesthetic — heavy clip art, dated form UI, corporate blue + gray + stock photography of people shaking hands. This is where the audience already lives and what they are tired of.

## Design Principles

1. **Speak their language, not SaaS.** Use surety-specific terms — bond forms, power of attorney, indemnity agreement, court cover sheet, non-cancellable bonds, renewal dates. Every sentence should read like it was written by someone who has actually typed a guardianship bond into a Word form at 4 PM on a Friday.

2. **Earn trust through specificity, not credentials.** Claims like "30 minutes saved per bond" or "Florida court bonds: probate, guardianship, VA fiduciary" land harder than any trust badge. Precision is the credibility signal.

3. **One job, done completely.** The page captures. Every design decision, every extra element, every color choice is evaluated against whether it helps or hinders a qualified agency owner clicking the CTA. Nothing else is in scope.

4. **Financial-grade visual restraint.** Mercury and Stripe visual language: deep navy as a structural anchor, teal and mint for action and emphasis, whitespace as breathing room, type as the primary visual element. No decorative clutter, no gradient-text, no glassmorphism.

5. **Show the pain before the relief.** The page structure mirrors the sales conversation: name the problem in their words first, then and only then introduce the solution. Visitors who feel understood stay; visitors who hit a pitch immediately leave.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best-effort accessibility targeting WCAG 2.1 AA as a working baseline — adequate contrast, keyboard navigation, screen-reader-friendly semantics. No formal audit required for launch. Mobile-first responsive layout is mandatory: most agency owners will arrive on a phone from a LinkedIn link.
